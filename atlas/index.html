        Data & method

        Chart. U.S. Billboard Year-End Hot 100 for 1959–2025, from annual transcriptions. 2026 is a project estimate from 48 weekly Hot 100 charts through November 28, 2026. It is drawn with dashed lines and hollow marks.

        Feelings. A language model labeled 53,950 lyric segments from 6,301 songs, reading each song’s full lyric for context. Each song’s share is the fraction of its lyric text, by character position, in each label. A year averages its annotated songs equally, so a song that charted in two years counts in both. Frustration counts as anger. Contempt, fear, gratitude, surprise, disgust, and neutral are grouped as “other.” These are model annotations of lyric text, not listener measurements.

        Coverage. Songs without usable lyrics are shown as plain grooves and excluded from the averages: instrumentals, missing transcripts, matches awaiting review, and compound singles. The gray bars under the line chart show each year’s annotated share.

        Listening. “Play on Spotify” links and cover art appear only for recordings matched in Hit Atlas’s audio table. Other songs offer a clearly labeled Spotify search.

              Table view

              Audio features come from ReccoBeats for the recording matched to each chart entry (audio_features.csv). Valence, energy, danceability, and acousticness are 0–1 model scores; tempo is in beats per minute and loudness in decibels. Audio is matched for 553 of the 600 entries from 2021 to 2026 but only a few dozen earlier ones, so this chapter starts in 2021. Lyric feelings come from Astra’s segment annotations in Track 01. A song’s main feeling is the label with the largest share of its lyric text. The correlation tile compares the four lyric feeling shares with valence, energy, danceability, and acousticness.
